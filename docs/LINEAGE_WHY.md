# LINEAGE — Why a trait helps or hurts

Purpose: every change the child sees gets a reason in a child's words (scope decision 60). This is the table the game reads its reasons from. Marc wrote the lines; the directions are the engine's own Classroom table (`lineage-classroom/src/config.js`, `PLACE_EFFECTS`, scope decision 55). `game/src/why.js` holds the same lines, and `game/test/why.test.js` checks that every "doesn't matter much" line is exactly where the engine's effect is 0, and that the three neutral traits never matter anywhere.

✓ the trait helps there, ✗ it hurts there, ~ it doesn't matter much there (the engine counts it as 0).

*2026-10-05, Round 9, scope decisions 100–105: Marc's simpler inheritance, a baby like its one parent and sometimes born with one small new difference, was built and measured with the gate on. It is not shipped: it missed the gate's targets, because without mates one family takes each place and a line held to the child's choices is crowded out. The table and its lines don't change with either inheritance. They say what a trait does in a place, not how it is passed on.*

*2026-10-02, Round 5, scope decision 72: six cells are "~" now, each where real animals of that place have the trait both ways, so the child's follows, not survival, decide it, and decide which animal the line becomes (`docs/LINEAGE_REAL_ANIMAL_REVEAL.md`). At the water's edge, thick fur (river otters and beavers have very thick fur; seals have short fur) and big eyes (seals have big eyes; otters, beavers and platypuses small ones), as Marc asked. In the high leaves, long back legs (squirrels have long ones; sloths climb on short ones) and a strong tail (squirrels have big bushy tails; koalas, sloths and slow lorises tiny ones). On open ground, thick fur (arctic foxes have very thick fur; cheetahs short fur) and a strong tail (cheetahs and jerboas have long tails; hares short ones). Each "~" line names a real animal of that place with the trait the other way. The lines they replace: thick fur ✓ on open ground "Thick fur keeps them warm on open ground." and ✗ at the water's edge "Thick, wet fur slows swimming."; long back legs ✓ in the high leaves "Long back legs help them leap between branches."; a strong tail ✗ in the high leaves "A heavy tail makes climbing harder." and on open ground "A heavy tail slows them down on land."; big eyes ✗ at the water's edge "Big eyes don't help underwater, and cost energy." Every other cell, and every direction that still helps or hurts, is as it was.*

|---|---|---|---|
| Webbed feet | ✗ "Webbing makes it hard to grip branches." | ~ "Webbed feet don't matter much on open ground." | ✓ "Webbed feet push through water." |

| Thick fur | ~ "Thick fur doesn't matter much in the trees." | ~ "Thick fur doesn't matter much on open ground: cheetahs have short fur." | ~ "Thick fur doesn't matter much in the water: seals have short fur." |
| Long back legs | ~ "Long legs don't matter much in the trees: sloths climb on short ones." | ✓ "Long back legs help them run fast." | ✗ "Long legs drag in the water." |
| Strong tail | ~ "A big tail doesn't matter much in the trees: koalas have tiny ones." | ~ "A big tail doesn't matter much on open ground: hares have short ones." | ✓ "A strong tail helps them swim." |
| Big eyes | ~ "Big eyes don't matter much in the trees." | ✓ "Big eyes spot things across open ground." | ~ "Big eyes don't matter much in the water: otters have small eyes." |
| Sleek body | ✗ "A sleek body is hard to climb with." | ~ "Body shape doesn't matter much on open ground." | ✓ "A sleek body slides through water." |

The neutral traits don't help or hurt anywhere:

- Coat: "Coat colour doesn't help or hurt anywhere."
- Ear tips: "Ear tip shape doesn't help or hurt anywhere."
- Tail tip: "The mark on the tail tip doesn't help or hurt anywhere."

A line always talks about the trait's far end ("webbed feet", "long legs"). The same line explains both ways of a variation: "Webbed feet push through water." is why more webbing does better at the water's edge, and why less webbing does worse there.

## Where the game uses the lines

- **A glowing baby's card** (scope decision 65, Round 3): any trait can be followed, with no hint whether it matters there; the card never gives a line before the child has tested the trait. (Before Round 3 a "~" or neutral trait couldn't be followed, and its card gave its line.)
- **After a follow, watching in real time** (scope decisions 67 and 68): the line bigger than last generation on a trait that helps there, "Your Mossfoot line with bigger eyes is growing." then the line from the table, "Big eyes spot things across open ground."; smaller on one that hurts there, "Your Mossfoot animals with smaller eyes are dying off." and its line. When a "Why?" comes that generation, the line from the table waits for the guess.
- **"About as well as your relatives."** A line on a "~" or neutral trait that did about as well as its relatives there (each grew within a quarter of the other, at 5 animals or more) gets a "Why?" guess, the first time on an iPad (scope decision 69; after that, "Your Mossfoot line is doing about as well as your relatives." and the trait's line, with no guess): for a "~", the trait's three place lines; for a neutral trait, "Ear tip shape helps them on the open ground.", "Ear tip shape hurts them on the open ground." and its line from the table, "Ear tip shape doesn't help or hurt anywhere." (Before scope decision 68, a fair test whose sides were within 2 ended in "About the same.")
- **A followed line dying out** (scope decisions 68 and 69): right there, "Why did your Mossfoot animals with smaller eyes die out?", with the followed trait's three place lines, when it hurts there and that is a new Field Guide discovery. Otherwise why is told, with no guess: the trait's line; its line and "Other traits decided who made it." when it doesn't matter there; what else set them apart when it helps there (the line's biggest hurter there, or the trait the others there had more of). Then "They didn't make it. Back to your line."
- **Every change of the family's size** (the narration): what helps and what hurts the family in its place. The family "has" a trait when its animals there average at least halfway to the far end (`HAS_AT`, 0.5).
  - Growing: its biggest helper's line, then "But …" its biggest hurter's: "Webbed feet push through water." "But long legs drag in the water."
  - Shrinking, most of it crowded out: the helper's line, then "But …" the hurter's. With no hurter, the trait that most set the family's animals that died apart from the survivors where they lived (by 0.1 or more of its average): "Long back legs help them run fast." "Others here have longer back legs." (or, for a trait that hurts, "The ones that died had longer back legs."). Often no one trait did: the ones that died were a little less suited in many small ways.
  - Lines that are not from the table, when the table has no reason: "Lots of babies were born." (growing with no helper), "Some were old and died." (most of the family's deaths were of old age), "The place is full, so some made room." (crowded out, and no trait sets the family apart).
- **See, guess, explain:** at a follow's result that goes the table's way (the line clearly growing on a ✓, clearly dying off on a ✗, about as well as its relatives on a "~" or neutral trait), and at a line dying out on a ✗, the world waits and the child taps a guess, when it is a new Field Guide discovery on this iPad (scope decision 69). Any other "Why?" is told as a line, and a sudden drop of the line asks nothing: its reason is the generation's line. The three answers are one trait's lines for the three places, so the child has to think about where the animals live. Then why: "Yes! Big eyes spot things across open ground." or "Good thinking. But here, big eyes spot things across open ground."
- **Predictions:** the reasonable answer gives the line for the line's place: "Grow. Long back legs help them run fast." or "Your line. Long back legs help them run fast." (`docs/LINEAGE_PREDICTION_QUESTIONS.md`). (Until scope decision 68, "Will it be passed on?" also used the lines: "It wasn't passed on. Long legs drag in the water.")
- **Helping here / Hurting here:** under the generation panel, what the family has that helps or hurts where it lives: "Helping here: webbed feet, a sleek body." "Hurting here: long legs." "Hurting here" shows only when it names a trait (scope decision 64): families soon lose what hurts where they live, so it is hidden almost all the time.
